# microsoft-365-not-a-backup
Why Microsoft 365 is not a backup and how ZIA Networks helps protect your business data.
*Your files live in the cloud, but that doesn't mean they're protected.*

Many businesses assume Microsoft 365 backs up their data. Emails, OneDrive files, SharePoint sites, and Teams chats all sit in Microsoft's cloud, so they must be safe. At **ZIA Networks**, we hear this assumption almost every week, and most companies only learn the truth after something goes wrong.

## Availability Is Not the Same as Backup

Microsoft keeps its services running. If a data center fails, your mailbox stays online. That is availability, and Microsoft is very good at it.

A backup is different. It is a separate, independent copy of your data that you can restore when something is deleted, corrupted or encrypted. Microsoft does not provide that by default.

Microsoft explains this through its shared responsibility model. Microsoft looks after the infrastructure and the service. You are responsible for your data, your users, and your access.

## What Can Go Wrong With Your Microsoft 365 Data

1. **Accidental deletion.** Someone deletes a folder, a mailbox or a whole SharePoint site. Deleted items sit in a recycle bin for a limited time, and after that they are gone for good. Retention windows differ by plan, so check Microsoft's current documentation.
2. **Ransomware.** Malware can encrypt files in OneDrive and SharePoint, and the damage syncs across devices. Version history helps, but it is not guaranteed to save you at scale.
3. **Malicious insiders.** A departing employee can delete or change large amounts of data on the way out. Microsoft sees it as a legitimate user action, so nothing stops it.
4. **Compromised accounts.** If an attacker takes over an admin or user account, they can delete data, change permissions, and cover their tracks.
5. **Sync errors and overwrites.** A bad sync or a wrong bulk edit can replace good files with bad ones, and the bad version spreads everywhere.
6. **Retention gaps and legal requests.** Retention policies are not backups. If a policy is set up wrong, or you need old data for an audit or legal case, you may not have it.

## "But I Have a Recycle Bin and Version History"

These are useful, but they are safety nets for small mistakes, not for large-scale loss. They have time limits; they can be wiped by the same person or attacker who caused the problem, and restoring many items at once is slow and manual.

A real backup is stored separately, kept as long as you decide, and restorable to a precise point in time.

## What a Good Microsoft 365 Backup Looks Like

- **Independent of Microsoft:** stored outside your tenant so one compromised account cannot erase everything.
- **Full coverage:** Exchange, OneDrive, SharePoint and Teams.
- **Automatic and frequent:** several backups a day, not a manual job someone forgets.
- **Long retention:** months or years, based on your needs and legal requirements.
- **Granular restore:** bring back one email or one file without rolling back everything.
- **Protected from deletion:** immutable copies so ransomware cannot touch them.
- **Tested:** a backup you have never restored is only a hope.
